Paste a clinical note and watch every HIPAA Safe Harbor identifier come out of it, category by category — then read the re-identification risk that survives. Built for product managers who have to defend a de-identification pipeline to compliance.

01  How it works

Six passes, no black box.

Every rule below runs in your browser on the text in the box. Nothing is sent anywhere and nothing is pre-computed.

Step 01
Layered detection against §164.514(b)(2). Three detector families run over the raw text: deterministic regular expressions for structured identifiers, gazetteers for names, cities and states, and context rules that fire on a trigger phrase and redact only the value that follows it — so MRN: survives and the number does not.
Step 02
Checksum validation, not shape-matching. Candidates are confirmed arithmetically where a published check exists: VIN position-9 check digit (ISO 3779), DEA registrant checksum, NPI Luhn over the 80840 prefix, CMS MBI positional format, and the SSA issuance rules that bar area 000, 666 and 900–999. A candidate that fails its check is still redacted, but drops to lower confidence and says so.
Step 03
Span resolution. Overlapping candidates are resolved by rule specificity, then span length — so a full street address wins over the bare road name inside it, and a validated MBI wins over the generic alphanumeric-code net. Suppressed candidates are counted, not silently dropped.
Step 04
Transformation under the selected method. Safe Harbor §164.514(b)(2) strips all 18 categories, keeps the initial three ZIP digits unless the prefix is on the restricted-population list, keeps years, and aggregates ages over 89 to 90+. Limited Data Set §164.514(e)(2) removes only the 16 enumerated direct identifiers and retains full dates, city, state and 5-digit ZIP.
Step 05
Residual risk by equivalence class. The surviving quasi-identifiers — geography, sex, birth granularity — are multiplied against a stated population denominator to estimate k, the expected number of people sharing the released combination. Every constant in that arithmetic is printed on the page.
Step 06
Seeded human-review queue. A reproducible sample of the weakest detections is drawn with a mulberry32 PRNG seeded from a hash of the input, so two reviewers looking at the same note get the same queue. Nothing on this page calls the platform’s unseeded random source.

05  Residual risk

Safe Harbor is not zero-risk.

De-identification removes identifiers. It does not remove the quasi-identifiers that let an adversary link a record back to a person through an external register — a voter roll, an obituary, a local news report.

Bands follow common de-identification practice: k < 5 is treated as high risk, and k ≥ 11 as low, matching the CMS cell-size suppression convention that cells of 10 or fewer are too small to release. A low band here means the modelled combination is common — not that the record is safe. Safe Harbor is a regulatory safe harbor, not a mathematical guarantee; HHS's own de-identification guidance describes the residual risk as very small but explicitly non-zero, and offers Expert Determination under §164.514(b)(1) as the alternative route.

06  Human review queue

Where a person still has to look.

A reproducible sample of the weakest detections, drawn with a seeded PRNG so the same note always produces the same queue. Confirm each one before release.

07  Method & limitations

What this catches, and what it does not.

What it catches. Identifiers with deterministic shape — SSN, telephone, fax, email, URL, IPv4 and IPv6, ZIP and dates in six formats — plus checksum-validated VIN, DEA, NPI and CMS MBI values, context-anchored MRN, plan, account, licence and device numbers, and names from a — gazetteer with title, relation and signature rules.

What it misses. Names outside the gazetteer with no title or relation cue; misspellings, OCR noise and nicknames; dictation without punctuation; non-US formats; and most of category 18, whose characteristics no regular expression can enumerate. Ambiguity-prone surnames (Ward, Green, King) are excluded to protect precision. Payer and facility names are not enumerated identifiers and are kept, though in a small catchment they re-identify. Ambiguous numeric dates are read US-first.

What production adds. A clinical NER model fine-tuned on an annotated corpus such as i2b2/UTHealth, ensembled with these rules rather than replacing them; a per-institution term list; surrogate generation instead of bracket tokens; and measured recall on a held-out gold set, because an unmeasured pipeline is an unquantified breach.

Where a human stays. Every low-confidence span, every category-18 flag, and every note reported clean. The restricted ZIP list is the 17-prefix HHS list from Census 2000; refresh it before real use.
